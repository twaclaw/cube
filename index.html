Enter a cube

Each menu offers only the colors that still leave a cube that can be finished, so the grid cannot be painted into a dead end, and anything that follows from what you have entered is filled in for you. Letters name faces, not colors: U is whatever your centre face is. The centres are fixed, since they are what name the six colors.

What this is

A Rubik's cube that lives entirely in the browser: no server, and nothing to install. The geometry, the solver and every drawing are written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly using [trunk](https://trunk-rs.github.io/trunk/).

Three views of one state

The 3D view is a perspective projection you can drag to rotate and zoom, with named camera presets for reproducible figures. The net is the cube unfolded flat, optionally with facelet numbers, and you can ring any set of facelets by clicking them or by typing a list such as 1,3,8-11. The permutation panel writes the state in disjoint cycle notation, each cycle saying where a facelet travels. The numbering, the net layout and the colors follow SageMath's sage.groups.perm_gps.cubegroup.

Entering a cube

The edit button opens a cube editor, either as the 54-letter string the query parameter carries or as an unfolded grid with a menu on every facelet. A cube is not 54 free choices: the six centres name the colors, and the other 48 stickers come in the twenty cubies the puzzle is built from, each used exactly once. So every menu offers only the colors that still leave a cube that can be finished -- which is checked by matching every unfinished slot against every unused piece, not by looking at one sticker at a time -- and anything that follows is filled in rather than asked for. Two stickers of a corner give the third; the last cubie of each kind follows from the ones before it. That leaves 36 facelets to enter at worst, and about 33 on average.

While the editor is open the other two views are twins of it: the 3D cube and the net both show the state as far as it has been entered, stickers still open left blank, and a sticker clicked in either one can be colored from the strip under the cube. The same cube, three ways of reaching it, so a sticker awkward to find on the flat grid can be turned to in three dimensions instead.

Solving

The solve button runs Kociemba's two-phase algorithm, which reaches the solved state in about twenty moves. Phase one takes the cube into the subgroup H = <U, D, R2, L2, F2, B2> and phase two finishes inside it. There is more on how and why it works in Magic Squares and Cubes.

Notation

The parser reads WCA notation (R2 U F'), the SageMath product (R^2*U*F^-1), and the crammed macro language of Tom Davis's Rubik, where rfu means R' F' U'. Slices, wide turns, whole-cube rotations and repeated groups such as (R U)3 all parse, and the notations can be mixed in one line. Cubes from 2x2 to 7x7 can be drawn and turned; the solver is for the 3x3.

Exporting the drawings and animations

Every view exports as SVG: a still, the flat net, a whole maneuver as a grid of labeled steps, an animated SVG that plays inside an <img> tag, or a self-contained HTML player. The embed snippet gives you an <iframe> of this page, state and all, still interactive. The same renderer is a command line tool and a Python module, which is how the figures in the marimo notebook are drawn.
